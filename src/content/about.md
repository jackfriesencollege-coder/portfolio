# About

I'm Jack Friesen, a mechanical engineering student at Colorado State University,
where I'm a Walter Scott, Jr. Scholar and part of the Honors College. I expect to
graduate in December 2028.

Most of what I do comes down to the same thing: take a physical system, figure out
how it actually behaves, and build something that measures or controls it.

## Research

At the **Powerhouse Energy Institute** I work on wildfire emissions research. My
most recent project there was the control system for a 225 L/min bioaerosol air
sampler — a custom PCB designed in KiCad, a watertight enclosure, and a tuned PID
loop that holds the sampler at a target flow rate using live pressure and flow
sensor data.

Before that, through CSU's **SURE Research** program, I worked with graduate
students and faculty on a mid-IR optical sensing system for early fire detection,
isolating the 2 µm and 4 µm bands with photodiodes, lenses, and prisms.

I also spent a semester on a four-person team designing a **passive thermosiphon
solar still**, using OpenFOAM to validate that convection alone would drive the
flow. It produced about 3 L/hour from under $60 of hardware-store parts.

## Before the lab

I got here through cars and tools, not textbooks.

I spent two years at **Discount Tire**, finishing as a crew chief, where the job
was keeping 30+ cars an hour moving through the bays — balance, bead seating,
seized lugs, and the occasional problem nobody wanted to touch. I've since rebuilt
the cylinder head on a 2001 Honda CR-V after a timing belt failure bent the valves,
and overhauled a car's A/C system down to the compressor, expansion valve, and
condenser.

I also worked as a **sprinkler technician** at Resource Central, diagnosing and
repairing irrigation systems and controllers — which was as much customer service
as it was hydraulics.

That background is the reason I'm comfortable with hardware that doesn't work yet.
