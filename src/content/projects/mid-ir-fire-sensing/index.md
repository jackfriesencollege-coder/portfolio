## The problem

Fire has a distinctive infrared signature, but picking it out of everything else
that emits in the same region is the hard part. Detecting it early — and without
the false alarms that make a detector useless — means looking at specific,
narrow wavelength bands rather than broad IR intensity.

## What I did

Through CSU's **SURE Research** program, I worked alongside graduate students and
research faculty to design and prototype a **mid-infrared optical sensing system**.

The system isolates the **2 µm and 4 µm** wavelengths using a combination of
photodiodes, lenses, and prisms, so the detector responds to the bands that
distinguish combustion rather than to IR generally.

## Result

A working benchtop prototype, and a considerably better understanding of how
optical design decisions — element choice, geometry, alignment — propagate into
what a sensor can and can't tell you.
